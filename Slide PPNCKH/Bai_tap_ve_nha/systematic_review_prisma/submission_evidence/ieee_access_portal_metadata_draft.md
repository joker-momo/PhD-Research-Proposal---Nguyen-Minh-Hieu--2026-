## Abstract

Collaborative deep-neural-network (DNN) inference can distribute computation across an unmanned-aerial-vehicle (UAV) swarm, but its benefit depends on model placement, intermediate-feature communication, mobility, and failure handling. This PRISMA-guided systematic review synthesizes full-text evidence published from 2021 onward on partitioning, orchestration, and resilience in multi-UAV inference. A protocol and two scope amendments were fixed before full-text synthesis. From a priority corpus of 20 verified reports, 17 full texts were retrieved and assessed; one two-page demonstration report was excluded, leaving 16 studies. Fifteen studies used simulation and one used a four-device proof-of-concept. Thirteen reported latency as a performance outcome, six evaluated energy, two directly evaluated node unavailability, and three implemented rescheduling or migration mechanisms. Five studies reported repeated-run aggregation or uncertainty information, and no inspected study identified a public implementation artifact. One study coupled NS-3 with PyTorch, but none evaluated an integrated flight–network stack based on ROS 2, PX4, or Gazebo. Heterogeneous objectives, models, network assumptions, and outcome definitions precluded meta-analysis; therefore, evidence was synthesized narratively and mapped across architecture, decision scope, evaluation realism, and methodological quality. The evidence supports conditional rather than universal benefits: collaboration helps when additional compute outweighs activation-transfer and coordination overhead, while sparse load or weak connectivity can favor local execution. The principal gaps are integrated flight–network experimentation, empirical failure recovery, uncertainty reporting, and reproducible artifacts. Because three priority reports and formal database exports remain unreconciled, the reported PRISMA flow is explicitly interim.
